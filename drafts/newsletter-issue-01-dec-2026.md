**Subject:** Ozaukee sold more homes in October. Washington County sold fewer.

**Preheader:** Plus the $2 million Firemen's Park vote, and what Cedarburg looks like heading into winter.

# 103 homes

That's how many homes sold in Ozaukee County in October, according to the Greater Milwaukee Association of REALTORS. That's up 8.4% from 95 a year ago, while sales across the four county Milwaukee metro fell 3.5%.

Hi, I'm Anthony Stolp. Welcome to the first issue of The River Report. Once a month I'll send you what's actually happening in Ozaukee and Washington County real estate: a little local news, the numbers that matter, and what they mean for your house. Every number has a source. No sales pitch.

## Around the two counties

**Firemen's Park could soon belong to the county.** The county plans to offer the Cedarburg Firemen's Association $2 million, its asking price, for Firemen's Park north of downtown Cedarburg. That's about 16 acres, including the old concrete grandstand. Put together with the county's land next door, it forms the 39 acre site for the free county fair every August. The Executive Committee was set to take up the resolution on Monday, Nov. 30, and the full County Board is expected to vote **Wednesday, Dec. 2**. A county memo puts the cost of tearing down the grandstand and improving the site at about another $3 million. The fire department told the county the land would likely be worth more to a developer, but it would rather keep the fair where it is. Nothing is final until the board votes, and nobody would guess at a closing date. (Ozaukee Press, Nov. 24, 2026)

My take: the fair, Maxwell Street Days and a walkable downtown are a big part of why people pay up to live in Cedarburg. Keeping that land in public hands is good news for the neighborhoods around it. I'll share the result next month.

**Washington County prices kept climbing.** The median sale price in Washington County was $420,000 in October, up 10.5% from $380,000 a year earlier. Sales fell to 139 from 156, and inventory held at 3.6 months. Year to date, the median is $419,500, up 8.1%. Fewer homes sold, but the ones that did sold for more. If you own in West Bend, Hartford or Germantown, that's still a good spot to be in. (Wisconsin REALTORS Association, October 2026 report)

## The numbers

**Ozaukee County, October 2026**

| | October 2026 | October 2025 | Change | Source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Homes sold | 103 | 95 | up 8.4% | GMAR (Metro MLS data) |
| Listings | 150 | 122 | up 23.0% | GMAR (Metro MLS data) |
| Median sale price | $475,000 | $485,950 | down 2.3% | WRA |
| Months of inventory | 3.8 | 3.8 | no change | WRA |
| Median, year to date | $505,000 | $479,900 | up 5.2% | WRA |

Why two sales numbers? GMAR counted 103 Ozaukee sales and WRA counted 97. GMAR closes its monthly count on the 10th of the next month, so the two reports never line up exactly. Both point the same way: more homes are selling in Ozaukee, and more are listed.

**Cedarburg right now.** Realtor.com's Cedarburg numbers for November 2026 show 56 homes for sale, up 26% from a year ago. The median listing price is $499,900, down about 15% from a year earlier. Homes are selling in a median of 31 days at about 98% of asking. I broke down the rest in my [Cedarburg market update for November](https://anthonystolp.com/blog/cedarburg-market-nov-2026).

**Rates.** The 30 year fixed rate averaged 7.33% in October, up from 6.25% a year earlier. (WRA)

**What it means if you're selling.** You have more competition than you did a year ago, especially in Cedarburg. Price your home to what actually sold nearby, not to what a neighbor got last spring. A well priced, well prepped home still sells in a few weeks.

**What it means if you're buying.** You have more to choose from than earlier this year, and there's a little room to negotiate (98% of asking, not 102%). The good ones still go fast, so get your financing lined up before you fall for a house.
