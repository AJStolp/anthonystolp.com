### The two size traps that will bite if nobody checks

**Trap 1: a 6 x 9 card is NOT EDDM eligible.** EDDM requires a flat-size piece. Per
[DMM 201](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/201.htm) a piece is flat-size only if it is more than 11-1/2
inches long, **or more than 6-1/8 inches high**, or more than 1/4 inch thick. At exactly 6 inches high a
6 x 9 card fails all three and is letter-size. The smallest common card that clears the bar is 6.25 x 9.
This is precisely why every EDDM vendor defaults to 6.5 x 9.

**Trap 2: retail and commercial postcard dimensions are different.** From Notice 123 p.36:

| | Max length | Max height | Thickness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Postcard, **retail** ($0.65) | 6 in | **4-1/4 in** | 0.007 to 0.016 in |
| Postcard, **commercial** ($0.453+) | 9 in | **6 in** | 0.007 to 0.016 in |

So a 6 x 9 card is too big to stamp at the counter for 65 cents, but fits the commercial card rate at
exactly the maximum and can go at $0.453 through a mail house. Also note the 0.016 inch thickness ceiling
rules out heavy 16pt-plus stocks at either card rate.

### 2.1 Title company farm lists: free, and that is the problem

Title companies serving the Milwaukee metro offer agents property data platforms with farm export.
Knight Barry Title, a large Wisconsin title company, references a "KBT Toolbox" and search packages at
[knightbarry.com](https://www.knightbarry.com/), though the feature set and Ozaukee coverage are not
documented publicly and would need a call. First American's TitleToolbox and DataTree are the national
equivalents. The mechanics are well known: the rep sets up an account, the agent exports labels, no
invoice is ever issued.

**The cost is regulatory, not financial.** Under RESPA:

> "No person shall give and no person shall accept any fee, kickback or other thing of value pursuant to
> any agreement or understanding, oral or otherwise, that business incident to or part of a settlement
> service involving a federally related mortgage loan shall be referred to any person."
>
> 12 CFR 1024.14(b), [law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1024.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1024.14)

"Thing of value" is defined expansively and the definition reaches this fact pattern by name:

> "It includes, without limitation, monies, things, discounts, salaries, commissions, fees, duplicate
> payments of a charge, stock, dividends, ... **services of all types at special or free rates**, sales
> or rentals at special prices or rates ..."
>
> 12 CFR 1024.14(d), emphasis added

The safe harbour is narrow and has **two** conditions, not one:

> "Normal promotional and educational activities that are **not conditioned on the referral of business**
> and that **do not involve the defraying of expenses that otherwise would be incurred** by persons in a
> position to refer settlement services or business incident thereto"
>
> 12 CFR 1024.14(g)(1)(vi), emphasis added

Read that second condition against the facts. A mailing list is an expense AJ would otherwise incur. He
is quoted 8 cents a record for it in the table above. A title company handing him that list for free
defrays an expense he would otherwise pay, which puts it outside the promotional-activities safe harbour
on its face, regardless of whether anyone says the word "referral" out loud. The absence of an explicit
quid pro quo does not cure it, because (g)(1)(vi) requires **both** conditions to be met.

Enforcement in this area has historically targeted the settlement service provider rather than the agent.
That is cold comfort. The agent side of a Section 8 arrangement is also a "person" who "shall not accept".

**Recommendation: do not build the farm on a free title list.** Sixteen dollars for a 200-record list is
not worth a RESPA conversation, and it is certainly not worth being the agent named in one. If AJ wants
the title relationship, buy the list and let the rep earn the order on service instead.

### 2.2 Ozaukee County public records: free, genuinely usable, but not free of labour

**Wisconsin Statewide Parcel Map Initiative.** The statewide parcel layer is free to download and
individual county extracts are published as shapefile and file geodatabase, so Ozaukee can be pulled on
its own. Source: [sco.wisc.edu/parcels/data](https://www.sco.wisc.edu/parcels/data/). The page carries a
"no warranty, expressed or implied" disclaimer. Schema documentation is a separate per-version PDF, and
field-level confirmation that both situs address and owner mailing address are present was **not
verified**. AJ should open `V12_Wisconsin_Statewide_Parcels_Schema_Documentation.pdf` before relying on it.

**Ozaukee County directly.** The county runs an Ascent land records portal at
`ascent.co.ozaukee.wi.us/LandRecords/` and a GIS hub at `ocgeohub-ozaukee.hub.arcgis.com`, per the
[Land Information Office page](http://www.ozaukeecounty.gov/163/Land-Information-Office). No published
fee schedule for bulk parcel extracts could be found. That is a phone call to 262-284-9411.

**Why absentee filtering works here, and why it matters.** Parcel data carries the owner's mailing
address separately from the property's situs address. Where they differ, the owner does not live there.
That one comparison separates owner-occupied from absentee without paying anyone for a flag. It is the
strongest argument for the public-records route.

**The labour is the real price.** Raw assessor data is not a mailing list. It arrives with trusts and
LLCs as owner names, no CASS standardisation, no USPS deliverability validation, no NCOALink move update,
duplicate parcels per owner, and no suppression of anything. Turning a 1,000-parcel export into a mailable
file is a few hours of spreadsheet work the first time, and the output is still less deliverable than a
vendor list. Buying those 1,000 records costs $80. AJ's time is worth more than $80 for three hours.

**Verdict: right choice for building a durable multi-year farm database AJ owns. Wrong choice for a
mailer that has to be in the mail nine days after a September 4 close.**

### 2.4 Suppression: the honest answer

**There is no legally binding do-not-mail list in the United States.** USPS does not operate one and
nothing in the DMM provides for one.

**DMAchoice**, run by the ANA, not USPS
([dmachoice.org](https://www.dmachoice.org/static/consumer_choice_tools.php)):

- Costs the **consumer** $8 online or $9 by mail for a 10-year registration
- **Voluntary.** ANA "provide[s] this suppression file to interested, responsible companies" and "calls
  on all marketers to review and follow the ANA industry standard guidelines". No statutory duty to honour it
- **Explicitly excludes unaddressed mail**, so an EDDM saturation drop is unaffected by DMAchoice
  opt-outs entirely. Only an addressed list mailing would be
- Also excludes companies the consumer transacted with in the last two years, local independent
  retailers, and political mail

**Move Update is mandatory, and it is not optional the way suppression is**
([DMM 602](https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/602.htm)):

- Applies to commercial First-Class presort or automation prices and to all USPS Marketing Mail prices.
  It is a **condition of the discount**
- Addresses must be updated **within 95 days before the mailing date**
- Approved methods include NCOALink and Address Change Service
- Above a 0.5 percent change-of-address error threshold, each offending piece is assessed **$0.08**

This is the sharpest practical argument for a vendor list over a county parcel export: vendor lists arrive
CASS-certified and NCOA-processed, parcel exports do not.

**Practical suppression for AJ is manual and short:** remove the subject property, remove the sellers,
remove anyone who has asked to be left alone, remove obvious non-residential parcels. Single digits.
Keep it in one place and apply it to every drop.

### 3.3 EDDM versus targeted: where the crossover actually sits

Per-piece, EDDM looks like a rout: **$0.48 versus $1.04**, a ratio of 2.17 to 1.

That comparison is wrong, and here is why. **EDDM is priced per delivery on the route, not per address you
wanted.** You cannot buy 200 specific homes. You buy every mailbox the carrier passes. So the real
comparison is:

- Targeted: `(addresses you want) x $1.04`
- EDDM: `(total deliveries on the routes covering those addresses) x $0.48`

Setting those equal:

> **EDDM wins only when the carrier route contains fewer than about 2.17 times your intended audience.**

To reach 200 owner-occupied homes:

| Route size | EDDM cost | Targeted cost | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 deliveries | $144 | $216 | EDDM |
| 434 deliveries | $208 | $216 | breakeven |
| 500 deliveries | $240 | $216 | Targeted |
| 700 deliveries | $336 | $216 | Targeted |

USPS residential city routes commonly run several hundred deliveries, so a single-neighbourhood just-sold
drop will usually land on the targeted side of that line. **AJ must pull the actual route counts at
[eddm.usps.com](https://eddm.usps.com/eddm/customer/routeSearch.action) before this is decided.** The
formula turns that one number into an answer.

**Where EDDM does not win at any price:**

- It cannot exclude renters. In a seller-lead message renters are pure waste, and they are a real fraction
  of any residential route.
- It cannot exclude absentee owners, so "your neighbour just sold" lands on people who do not have a
  neighbour.
- It cannot be addressed by name. Every piece reads "Postal Customer", which reads as junk.
- It forces a flat-size piece over 6-1/8 inches high. The cheap 4.25 x 5.5 card is off the table, and so
  is a 6 x 9.
- Sub-route geography is impossible. A just-sold card is inherently about the twenty houses that can see
  the sign. That is a fraction of a route, and it is exactly what EDDM cannot do.
- Marketing Mail speed, with no USPS-published day band. On a "this just happened" piece that is a real cost.

**Where EDDM does win:** a wide, undifferentiated brand or market-report drop across the full farm at
several thousand pieces, where the message works for owners and renters alike and precision is not the
point. Different campaign.

**Verdict for the September mailer: targeted First Class, not EDDM. The precision is the product.**

## 4. The Wisconsin compliance floor

### 4.1 Where the rule actually lives

`Wis. Admin. Code REEB 24.04` was repealed and recreated effective 2022-04-01 and is now a bare pointer:

> "**REEB 24.04 Advertising.** A licensee shall follow the requirements for advertising as specified under
> s. 452.136, Stats."
>
> [docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/reeb/24](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/reeb/24)
> (history: `CR 21-043: r. and recr. Register March 2022 No. 795, eff. 4-1-22`)

The operative rules are statutory, in **Wis. Stat. 452.136**, at
[docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/452.136](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/452.136).

Worth flagging because older guidance still circulates: the phrase **"in a manner reasonably calculated to
attract attention" is not current Wisconsin law.** It was pre-2017 REEB 24.04 language superseded by 2017
Wis. Act 110. **The current standard is "clear and conspicuous."**

### 4.2 What must appear on the piece

> **452.136(1) FALSE ADVERTISING.** "A licensee may not advertise in a manner that is false, deceptive,
> or misleading."

> **452.136(2)(a)** "Except for advertisements for the rental of real estate owned by the licensee, a
> licensee shall in all advertising disclose the firm's name **exactly as printed on the license** of the
> licensed individual broker or licensed broker business entity or disclose a trade name previously filed
> by the firm with the department and shall in either case **clearly indicate that the firm is a business
> enterprise and not a private party**."

> **452.136(2)(b)** "... a licensee associated with a firm shall advertise **under the supervision of and
> in the name of the firm**. The firm's name as used in advertising shall be **clear and conspicuous**."

Emphasis added. **There is no stated prominence ratio.** WRA reports the board decides case by case: "The
REEB has discretion to determine if the firm's name is clear and conspicuous in the advertisement."
(Cori Lamont, [Five Things to Know About the New State Advertising Rules](https://www.wra.org/WREM/Jan18/Advertising/), Jan. 2018.)

**Wisconsin does not require the individual salesperson's name to appear.** 452.136(2) runs entirely to
the firm's name. The only place self-identification as a licensee is mandated is 452.136(2)(c), which
covers a licensee selling their own property and does not apply here. NAR Article 12 separately requires
that AJ's status as a real estate professional be readily apparent, and that is the binding source for
putting his own name and role on the card.

**Not yet in force:** 452.136(1m), requiring disclosure where advertising "has been altered or modified
using technology, including artificial intelligence, to add, remove, or change elements of the property",
was created by 2025 Wis. Act 69 and **takes effect 2027-01-01**. It does not govern a September 2026
postcard. It will govern the drone and AI-processed imagery strategy from January, so build the habit now.

### 4.3 Advertising a sale that was not AJ's listing. This is the one that bites.

> **452.136(3) ADVERTISING WITHOUT AGENCY AGREEMENT PROHIBITED.** "A firm and any licensees associated
> with the firm may not advertise a property unless one of the following applies: **(a)** The firm is the
> listing firm for the property. **(b)** The firm or a licensee associated with the firm has obtained
> **consent to advertise the property from the listing firm** for the property."

AJ represented the buyer. Epique was not the listing firm. On the face of the statute, (b) is the only
available path and it requires the listing firm's consent.

Two things that must not be glossed over:

1. **The statute is textually silent on timing.** It says "may not advertise a property", with no carve-out
   for closed transactions and no expiry at closing. Whether a post-closing "just sold" postcard is
   "advertis[ing] a property" within (3) when the property is no longer for sale is a genuine interpretive
   question the text does not answer. No REEB order, case annotation, or WRA article resolving it was found.
2. **Do not assume MLS participation supplies the consent.** WRA notes that "the MLS provides the practical
   consent from the listing firm for the other firm to advertise the listing", but that is framed around
   advertising an **active listing**, not a sold-property mailer. It is not safe to extend.

WRA's own do-not list is explicit that this reaches postcards. Among the "don'ts": advertise "another
firm's listing without getting consent from the listing firm", and "advertise in a 'team' name without
including the firm's name". Among the "dos": "Include the firm's name in all advertising, including
business cards, websites, social media, **postcards** and all other advertising." (Jennifer Lindsley, WRA
staff attorney, [The Do's and Don'ts of Advertising](https://www.wra.org/WREM/Apr23/Advertising/), Apr. 2023.)

The NAR Code of Ethics is more permissive and resolves the "can I say sold" question cleanly:

> **Standard of Practice 12-7.** "Only REALTORS® who participated in the transaction as the listing broker
> or **cooperating broker (selling broker)** may claim to have 'sold' the property. **Prior to closing**, a
> cooperating broker may post a 'sold' sign only with the consent of the listing broker."

> **Standard of Practice 12-5.** "REALTORS® shall not advertise nor permit any person employed by or
> affiliated with them to advertise real estate services or listed property in any medium ... without
> disclosing the name of that REALTOR®'s firm in a reasonable and readily apparent manner."

> **Article 12.** "REALTORS® shall be honest and truthful in their real estate communications and shall
> present a true picture in their advertising, marketing, and other representations. REALTORS® shall
> ensure that their status as real estate professionals is readily apparent ..."

Source: [NAR Code of Ethics](https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/governing-documents/code-of-ethics).

As the buyer's agent AJ **was** the cooperating broker, so under NAR he **may** claim the property sold,
and 12-7's consent sentence is limited to sold signs **prior to** closing, which does not bite here.
**But NAR permission does not displace 452.136(3). The state statute is stricter and controls.**

**The clean resolution costs one email.** Ask the listing firm for written consent to reference the
property in post-closing marketing. Get it before September 4. It moots the interpretive question
entirely and costs nothing.

### 4.4 Two adjacent rules the brief did not ask about but that apply

**Client confidentiality survives closing.** Wis. Stat. 452.133(1)(d): the firm "shall continue to keep
the information confidential after the transaction is complete and after the firm is no longer providing
brokerage services to the party." Naming AJ's buyer client, or disclosing terms, on a mailer implicates
this. Sale price is generally public via the transfer return, but client identity and negotiated terms are
a different matter. **Confirm with the broker before naming the buyer.**

**False portrayal.** REEB 24.085: "No licensee shall draft or use any document that the licensee knows
falsely portrays an interest in real estate."
[Source](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/reeb/24/085). Narrow and document-focused, but a
card implying AJ held the listing sits close to it, and squarely inside 452.136(1).

**Discipline grounds**, for scale: Wis. Stat. 452.14(3)(d) lets the board revoke, suspend, limit or
reprimand a licensee who "pursued a continued and flagrant course of misrepresentation or made false
promises through other licensees or **through advertising**."

### 4.5 New risk: the MLS listing photos are probably not AJ's to use

WRA Legal Update 15.08 on real estate advertising content states that MLS terms of use "allow limited use
of the photographs of other MLS participants and subscribers. They **do not allow copying for future
listings or the advertising of sold property**." [wra.org/LU1508](https://www.wra.org/LU1508/).

That article predates 2017 Act 110, so its statutory content is stale, but the MLS-terms point is a
contract and copyright issue, not a statutory one, and it is directly on point for a just-sold card. The
listing firm or its photographer owns those images, not AJ.

**Current Metro MLS / WIREX rule text was not verified.** Two safe paths: use AJ's own photography of the
exterior, which he can shoot from the public right of way, or get image permission in the same email as
the 452.136(3) consent.

### 4.6 What does not apply

- **CAN-SPAM is email only.** 15 U.S.C. 7702 defines "electronic mail message" as one "sent to a unique
  electronic mail address". Nothing in the Act reaches postal mail.
- **Wis. Stat. 100.52 is telephone only.** Titled "Telephone solicitations", limited to "the unsolicited
  initiation of a telephone conversation or text message".
  [Source](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/100.52).
- **No Wisconsin state do-not-mail registry was found.** Reported as a negative search finding.
- **No FTC "ADVERTISEMENT" labelling requirement** for real estate direct mail. The general federal
  constraint is FTC Act Section 5 on deceptive practices, a truthfulness standard, not a labelling one.
- **No Wisconsin requirement to print "licensed in Wisconsin".** NAR SoP 12-9's state-of-licensure
  requirement applies to **firm websites**, not postal mail.
- One pointer, not independently verified against primary text: 39 U.S.C. 3001 restricts mail designed to
  simulate a government document, check or invoice. Avoid any "official notice" styling.

### 4.7 Compliance checklist for the piece

- [ ] **"Epique Realty" appears exactly as printed on the Wisconsin firm license**, or as a trade name
      filed with DSPS under REEB 23.03. **Confirm the exact string with the broker.** Could not be verified
      independently: the DSPS lookup at [license.wi.gov](https://license.wi.gov/s/license-lookup) is a
      Salesforce single-page app that returned 403 to a direct fetch. Epique's own site titles itself
      "Epique Realty, Inc" and its footer shows a **California** DRE number, which says nothing reliable
      about the Wisconsin licensed entity name.
- [ ] Firm name is **clear and conspicuous**, not 6pt type at the bottom edge. 452.136(2)(b).
- [ ] The piece **clearly indicates Epique Realty is a business enterprise, not a private party**.
      452.136(2)(a).
- [ ] **"ExSell Experts" never appears without "Epique Realty"** and does not out-shout it. No explicit
      Wisconsin team-name rule exists; this is derived from 452.136(2) and WRA guidance.
- [ ] AJ's **status as a real estate professional is readily apparent**. NAR Article 12.
- [ ] The piece **accurately states AJ represented the buyer**. Do not imply he held the listing.
      452.136(1), REEB 24.085, NAR SoP 12-7.
- [ ] **Written consent from the listing firm** to reference the property, obtained before September 4.
      452.136(3).
- [ ] **Image rights cleared.** Own photography, or written permission. Not MLS photos by default.
- [ ] **Buyer client not named**, and terms not disclosed, without broker sign-off. 452.133(1)(d).
- [ ] Sale price shown only if accurate and only post-closing. 452.136(1) and 452.136(4).
- [ ] Piece reviewed and approved by the Epique broker before the order is placed. 452.136(2)(b) requires
      advertising "under the supervision of" the firm.
- [ ] **Epique brand standards: no publicly documented agent marketing or brand policy could be found.**
      This is a **question for the broker**, not an assumption. Ask for logo files, required brokerage-name

- [ ] From 2027-01-01, disclose any AI or technology alteration of property imagery. 452.136(1m).

### 5.2 Working backward from the September 4 close

Closing is **Friday, September 4, 2026**. **Labor Day is Monday, September 7, 2026**, a federal holiday
with no mail processing, which eats the first business day after the close.

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **Fri Aug 28** | **HARD ART DEADLINE.** Final art proofed and broker-approved. List purchased and cleaned. Written consent from the listing firm in hand. Image rights cleared. |
| Mon Aug 31 to Thu Sep 3 | Buffer. Absorbs broker revisions, a slow consent reply, or a list problem. |
| **Fri Sep 4** | Closing. Sale price and date become factually assertable. |
| Mon Sep 7 | Labor Day. No processing. |
| **Tue Sep 8** | Order placed with final price and close date filled in. |
| **Wed Sep 9** | Printed and mailed. |
| **Fri Sep 11 to Wed Sep 16** | In mailboxes. |

That puts the card in hand 7 to 12 days after closing, the right window for a just-sold piece.

**Why August 28 and not later.** The deadline is not driven by print time, it is driven by the four things
that can stall and are not under AJ's control: broker approval, the listing firm's consent, image rights,
and list delivery. Print is one day. Those four are the risk. A week of buffer is the minimum that makes
the September 11 in-home date reliable rather than hopeful.

**If August 28 slips**, the last order date that still lands inside a two-week post-close window is Tuesday
September 15, mailing September 16, in home September 18 to 23. Past that the piece stops being news.

## 6. Cadence economics, and why 200 pieces once tells you nothing

### 6.1 The benchmarks, and how much to trust them

The industry reference is the **ANA Response Rate Report**, published by the Association of National
Advertisers, which absorbed the DMA. The report is member-gated and **could not be read primary**. Figures
below are SECONDARY, quoted by direct mail vendors who have an obvious interest in the number being high:
average direct mail response 4.4 percent, house lists 5 to 9 percent, prospect lists 2.0 to 4.4 percent,
and a "real estate" figure of 3.32 percent. Sources:
[mailpro.org](https://www.mailpro.org/post/direct-mail-response-rates/),
[CRST](https://blog.crst.net/direct-mail-response-rates/).

**Treat these as marketing material, not as a planning input.** Three problems:

1. Provenance is muddy. The same figures recycle across dozens of vendor blogs with the edition year
   varying, and the underlying report cannot be inspected.
2. "Response" in the ANA methodology is a cross-industry construct dominated by categories with cheap,
   immediate conversion actions. A coupon redemption and a listing appointment are not the same event.
3. A prospect-list farming postcard is the hardest case in that dataset, not the average one.

**The number AJ should plan against comes from NAR.** In the 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers,
**66 percent of sellers found their agent through a referral or used an agent they had worked with in the
past**
([Virginia REALTORS summary](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers/),
SECONDARY summary of a NAR primary report). Direct mail is fighting for a share of the remaining third,
against the internet, signs, and open houses. That, not 4.4 percent, is the constraint.

Planning assumption: **0.5 to 1 percent response on a prospect farm list is defensible.** Above that is a
pleasant surprise.

### 6.2 The statistics, made concrete

| Volume | @ 0.5% | @ 1% | @ 2% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 pieces | 1 response | 2 responses | 4 responses |
| 1,000 pieces | 5 responses | 10 responses | 20 responses |

Wilson score intervals at 95 percent confidence:

- **2 responses out of 200** is an observed 1.0 percent with an interval of roughly **0.28 to 3.6 percent**.
  The true rate could be one thirteenth of the estimate or three and a half times it. This measurement is
  worthless. It cannot distinguish a good campaign from a bad one.
- **10 responses out of 1,000** is 1.0 percent with an interval of roughly **0.55 to 1.8 percent**. Better,
  still a factor of three. Enough to say "it worked at all", not enough to compare two creatives.

Sample size to estimate a 1 percent response rate within plus or minus 0.5 percentage points at 95 percent:

```
n = 1.96² × 0.01 × 0.99 / 0.005²
  = 3.8416 × 0.0099 / 0.000025
  = 1,521 pieces
```

Halving that margin to plus or minus 0.25 points requires **6,085 pieces**.

**The defensible conclusion: a single 200-piece drop produces zero usable information about response rate.
It is not a test and should not be run as one.** Roughly **1,500 cumulative delivered pieces** is the floor
at which the measured rate carries any signal, and about 6,000 before it is precise enough to compare
variants. At 1,000 pieces a month that is a month and a half to first signal, six months to real precision.

### 6.3 Single send versus sequence

**No study with a published methodology on real estate farming touch counts could be located.** The widely
repeated "it takes 7 to 12 touches" figure appears throughout brokerage and vendor training material with
no traceable source. Label it industry convention, not evidence.

What the arithmetic above supports without needing a study:

- A single 200-piece send is a **brand impression, not a campaign.** Budget it as such: $216 to be
  remembered by a few hundred neighbours at the moment AJ has a genuine local proof point. That is a fair
  price and a good reason to send it. It is not a lead-generation test.
- Sequence is not a growth hack, it is a **statistical necessity.** The only way a 200-address neighbourhood
  produces a measurable response rate is by accumulating touches until the denominator means something.
  Twelve monthly touches to 200 addresses is 2,400 cumulative deliveries, clearing the 1,521-piece floor.
- Sellers move on an **11-year median tenure** (NAR 2025 Profile). In any 200-home neighbourhood roughly 18
  homes turn over per year, spread across all twelve months. A single September card is in front of maybe
  one or two of those decisions at the moment they are being made. Sustained presence is how you intersect
  the other sixteen.

### 6.4 Does the money work

Ozaukee County median sale price was **$510,000 in March 2026**, up 12.3 percent year over year, and
$511,100 in June 2026, per
[Redfin's Ozaukee County market data](https://www.redfin.com/county/3208/WI/Ozaukee-County/housing-market)
(SECONDARY).

Assuming a 2.5 percent commission on one side, which post-2024 is negotiated rather than standard and is
stated here purely as an assumption, one transaction at median grosses roughly **$12,750** before any
brokerage split.

At $1.04 per piece that gross buys **about 12,260 pieces**. So:

- 1,000 pieces per month for 12 months costs **$12,480**. One closed deal at county median roughly breaks
  even on gross commission, before splits.
- 200 pieces per month for 12 months costs **$2,592**. Needs roughly one deal every five years to break
  even, a low bar, but it also generates too few impressions to move anything.

**The honest read: a 1,000-address farm mailed monthly has to produce two to three closings a year to be
clearly worth the money, not one.** That is achievable for a well-chosen farm, and it is also why the farm
has to be chosen for turnover rate rather than proximity to AJ's house. Farm selection is a separate
decision and should be its own ticket.

### 6.5 What to actually do

1. **Send the September just-sold card to roughly 200 addresses.** $216. Justify it as a credibility
   impression at the one moment AJ has local proof, not as a test.
2. **Do not measure a response rate off it.** There is nothing to measure.
3. **Decide separately whether to commit to a 12-month farm.** If yes, 1,000 addresses monthly at roughly
   $1,040 a month, with the first real read on response arriving around month two.
4. Every piece drives to a tracked destination. The site already has `/n/[token]` offline attribution
   minting `anthonystolp_vid` and resolving offline context, which is exactly what a QR or short URL on a
   postcard needs. Use it, so the response rate is measured rather than guessed.
