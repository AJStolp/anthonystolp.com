# What 6 deals in the pipe actually costs in conversations

Research for issue #28, under map issue #27 (winter lead-gen operating system).
Compiled 2026-08-05. Subject: solo agent, full time, database under 50 people,
one sale closing 2026-09-04, farming Ozaukee County WI zips 53012, 53092, 53097,
53024, 53074, 53080.

---

## Headline

**13 new conversations per week, sustained through the Nov-Mar window, to hold a
minimum of 6 live deals in the pipeline.**

Plausible range: **8 to 20 per week.** Outside the winter window the same target
needs about **9 per week**.

A "new conversation" here means a two-way exchange with a person not previously
in the pipeline, long enough to learn their timeline and motivation. It is not a
dial, not a form fill, not a like. That distinction matters, because the
coaching-industry activity numbers usually count dials.

Before the chain, two results that arguably matter more than the headline:

1. **Holding 6 live deals at all times implies roughly 21 closed sides per year.**
   The median REALTOR closes 9 ([NAR 2026 Member
   Profile](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/even-in-a-tougher-market-realtors-are-holding-their-ground)).
   Six in the pipe is not a starter target, it is roughly 2.3x median production.
   Running the same model backwards, the median REALTOR holds about **2.6** live
   deals at any moment.
2. **21 sides a year is about 1% of every transaction side in Ozaukee County.**
   The county closed 1,104 homes in 2025 (Redfin county tracker, below), so
   roughly 2,208 sides exist to be won. The target is a market-share target, not
   an activity target, and it should be argued on those terms.

### Link 2. How long a signed client occupies a pipeline slot

| Component | Value | Source | Trust |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer median weeks searching | 10 weeks | [NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers highlights](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-11/2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers-highlights-11-04-2025.pdf), exhibit 3-3 | PRIMARY |
| Median days, contract to close | 30 days | [NAR REALTORS Confidence Index, June 2026](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2026-07/2026-06-realtors-confidence-index-07-08-2026.pdf), p.3 | PRIMARY |
| Ozaukee median DOM, winter | 51.4 days | Redfin county tracker, above | PRIMARY |
| Homes viewed, median | 7 | NAR Profile, 2025 edition. The 2026 edition dropped this metric | PRIMARY, prior cycle |

Buyer-side slot: 10 weeks of searching plus 30 days to close equals **14.3 weeks**.
This assumes the buyer agency agreement is signed at the start of the search,
which post-August-2024 practice generally requires before a showing.

Seller-side slot, using the local number rather than the national one: 51.4 days
on market plus 30 days to close equals 81.4 days, or **11.6 weeks**. Redfin's DOM
measures list date to pending, so adding contract-to-close on top is a valid sum
and not a double count.

Buyer/seller mix: **assumed 60% buyer, 40% seller.** This is an inference, not a
sourced figure. It rests on primary data that listings flow disproportionately
from relationships: 37% of sellers were referred by a friend, neighbor or
relative and another 29% used the agent before, 66% combined ([NAR 2026
Generational Trends
Report](https://assets.inman.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2026-Generational-Trends-Report-FINAL.pdf),
exhibit 7-1). With a database under 50 and one closing to date, the listing
supply is structurally constrained this winter, so the mix tilts buyer.

Weighted slot: 0.6 x 14.3 + 0.4 x 11.6 = **13.2 weeks**.

### Link 3. Attrition, and what it does to the slot

| Metric | Value | Source | Trust |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contracts terminated, past 3 months | 5% to 6% across 8 monthly readings, Dec 2024 to Jun 2026 | [NAR RCI Jun 2026](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2026-07/2026-06-realtors-confidence-index-07-08-2026.pdf), [NAR RCI Dec 2025](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2026-01/2025-12-realtors-confidence-index-01-21-2026.pdf) | PRIMARY |
| Contracts with delayed settlement | 13% to 16% over the same window | Same | PRIMARY |
| US listings delisted without selling or going pending | 5.8% of active listings in April 2026, near a post-2020 high | [Redfin, Jun 3 2026](https://www.redfin.com/news/press-releases/redfin-reports-sellers-are-pulling-their-homes-off-the-market-at-near-record-rates/) | PRIMARY |

The delisting figure is a **monthly** share of active inventory, not the share of
a listing cohort that never sells. Over a listing life of roughly 1.7 months
that implies on the order of 10% of a cohort dying, which is a derivation, not a
published number. **No primary source publishes an annual expired-listing rate.**
The widely repeated "30 to 40% of listings expire" traces to REDX, a lead vendor,
and is unverified.

Model: **10% of signed clients die before contract, at roughly half the normal
dwell, and 5.5% of the survivors terminate after contract.** Combined survival to
close is about **85%**.

Early deaths shorten the average slot: 0.9 x 13.2 + 0.1 x 6.6 = **12.6 weeks**, or
**2.90 months**.

### Link 4. Arrival rate, by Little's Law

To hold a queue of length L with average dwell W, arrivals must run at L divided
by W.

    L = 6 live deals
    W = 2.90 months
    arrivals = 6 / 2.90 = 2.07 newly signed clients per month

**Roughly 2 new signed clients per month, every month, without a gap.** A gap
does not delay the target, it drains the pipeline, because the slots empty on
their own schedule regardless of what enters.

### Link 5. Conversations per signed client, split by source

This is the weakest link in the chain and it is weak for a specific reason: the
middle of the real estate funnel is not measured by anyone credible.

**What does not exist, confirmed by looking:**

- **Cold internet lead to closed transaction.** Zillow names lead-to-transaction
  conversion as a material revenue accounting estimate and **withholds the
  value** in its [FY2025
  10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1617640/000161764026000015/z-20251231.htm).
  NAR's [2025 REALTORS Technology Survey](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf),
  the one NAR report that studies lead generation directly, contains **no
  conversion rate of any kind.** It reports lead-source shares and spend only.
- **Sphere or referral contact-to-appointment rate.** No source at any tier.
- **Appointment-to-signed-client rate.** No source at any tier, primary or vendor.
- **Open house visitor-to-client conversion.** No primary source. The sign-in
  vendors best placed to publish it publish nothing.
- **Dials or contacts per appointment, real estate specifically.** No source.

**A warning worth carrying forward:** the figure "0.4% to 1.2% conversion, per
NAR" is quoted across coaching and CRM sites. It appears in **no NAR document**.
It traces to "NAR data cited by Inman" and nothing further. Do not attribute it
to NAR. Separately, vendors routinely present NAR *share* statistics as
conversion rates, for example presenting "43% of buyers were referred to their
agent" as a referral conversion rate. It is not.

**What does exist**, by source type. Every rate below is an **UNVERIFIED INDUSTRY
CLAIM** stated as a range, with primary corroboration noted where any exists.

| Source type | Claimed rate | Range used | Where it comes from | Primary corroboration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sphere / referral | 15% to 25% lead to close | **15-25%, central 20%** | [conversionrealtor.com](https://conversionrealtor.com/conversion-research/real-estate-conversion-rate-benchmark) | 74% of buyers and 80% of sellers contact **only one agent** ([2026 Gen Trends](https://assets.inman.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2026-Generational-Trends-Report-FINAL.pdf), exhibits 4-6 and 7-2). A referred agent usually faces no competing interview |
| Cold internet lead | 0.4% to 5%, with 7-9% claimed for top teams | **1-6%, central 2%** | conversionrealtor.com, ylopo.com, placester.com, followupboss.com | None. Zillow withholds it, NAR has never published one |
| Open house sign-in | 5% to 10% | **5-10%, central 7%** | conversionrealtor.com | 5% of buyers and 3% of sellers found their **agent** at an open house; 48% of buyers used open houses as a search source (Gen Trends exhibits 4-4, 7-1, 3-2) |
| Direct mail / farm | 0.5% to 3% response in year one | **5-10% of a responder converts, central 7%** | blog.crst.net, jamilacademy.com, directmail.io | USPS: **3%** of households say they are very likely to respond to mail from a business with **no prior relationship** ([USPS Household Diary Study FY2025, filed with the Postal Regulatory Commission](https://prc.arkcase.com/api/prc-dockets/filing/downloadFile?fileId=252658&inline=true)), and USPS states plainly that stated intent overstates behavior. Outcome side: under 1% of buyers and 2% of sellers found their agent via direct mail (Gen Trends 4-4, 7-1) |

Two honest caveats on how these rates are applied.

The vendor ranges are **lead to close**. This model needs **conversation to
signed client**, which is a different pair of endpoints. Applying the rates
unchanged is conservative in one direction, since a conversation is a *contacted*
lead and its true rate is higher than a raw lead's, and slightly aggressive in
the other, since signed is not the same as closed. The two errors run opposite
and roughly offset. No adjustment factor was invented to bridge them.

The direct-mail rate above is applied to a **responder**, by analogy to open
house, since both are self-selected inbound contacts with local intent. That
analogy is an assumption, not a source.

### Link 6. Channel mix, and the constraint that sets it

Assumed winter mix of new conversations:

| Channel | Share | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Sphere / referral | **10%** | Database under 50, one closing to date |
| Cold internet, site funnel plus the 5,408-lead Lofty pond | **60%** | The only channel with volume already sitting in it |
| Open house | **20%** | The /property sign-in system already exists |
| Direct mail farm | **10%** | Budget available post-close |

**The sphere share is the whole ballgame, and it is capped by the database.**
Sphere converts at roughly 10x the cold rate, but a database under 50 people
cannot supply many *new* conversations. NAR's median REALTOR draws 20% of
business from repeat clients and 21% from past-client referrals ([2024 NAR
Member Profile
highlights](https://www.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/documents/2024-nar-member-profile-highlights-07-10-2024.pdf)),
rising to 42% and 29% for agents with 16 or more years. That engine runs on past
clients. With one closing, it is structurally near zero this winter, which is
exactly why the required conversation count is high.

Blended conversation-to-signed rate at the central estimates:

    0.10 x 20%  =  2.00%
    0.60 x  2%  =  1.20%
    0.20 x  7%  =  1.40%
    0.10 x  7%  =  0.70%
    blended     =  5.30%

### Link 7. The winter penalty

Ozaukee closes 32% fewer homes per month in Nov-Mar than in Apr-Oct (Link 1). A
conversation held in January is therefore less likely to belong to someone who
transacts inside the modeled window.

**Applying the transaction-volume ratio, 0.677, as a proxy for the seasonal
conversion penalty is an assumption, not a sourced adjustment.** No source
publishes a seasonal conversion rate. The proxy is stated so it can be rejected;
the no-penalty answer is given alongside it throughout.

    winter blended rate = 5.30% x 0.677 = 3.59%

### Link 8. The arithmetic

    conversations per signed client  = 1 / 0.0359 = 27.9
    signed clients needed per month  = 2.07
    conversations per month          = 27.9 x 2.07 = 57.7
    conversations per week           = 57.7 / 4.33 = 13.3

**13 new conversations per week.**

Without the winter penalty, at the same mix: 1 / 0.0530 = 18.9 conversations per
signed client, 39.1 per month, **9.0 per week.**

Range, moving all four conversion rates together to their published extremes:

| Scenario | Blended rate | Winter-adjusted | Conversations / week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic (15%, 1%, 5%, 5%) | 3.60% | 2.44% | **19.6** |
| Central (20%, 2%, 7%, 7%) | 5.30% | 3.59% | **13.3** |
| Optimistic (25%, 6%, 10%, 10%) | 9.10% | 6.16% | **7.8** |

**8 to 20 per week. Call it 13.**

## Sensitivity: what the answer is most fragile to

**The single most fragile assumption is the cold internet lead conversion rate.**
It carries 60% of the modeled mix, and it is the one number in the entire chain
with **zero primary support from anyone**, including the company whose business
model depends on it. The published range spans more than an order of magnitude.

Holding everything else at the central estimate and moving only that rate across
its published range:

| Cold internet rate | Blended | Conversations / week |
|---|---|---|
| 0.4% (low end of published claims) | 4.34% | **16.3** |
| 1% | 4.70% | **15.0** |
| **2% (central)** | **5.30%** | **13.3** |
| 5% | 7.10% | **10.0** |
| 8% (claimed top-team portal performance) | 8.90% | **7.9** |

One unsourced number moves the operating target from 8 per week to 16 per week.
Everything else in the chain would have to move by roughly 2.5x to matter as
much.

**Runner-up, and the one that is actually controllable:** the sphere share of the
mix. Raising sphere from 10% to 25% of conversations, taken out of cold, drops
the requirement from 13.3 to **8.8 per week**, a 34% cut, without changing a
single conversion rate. That is the largest lever available and it is the direct
argument for treating database growth and the September 4 closing as the winter's
highest-value work, not as a nice-to-have.

**Assumptions that barely matter, checked so they can be set aside:**

- Buyer/seller mix. Moving from 60/40 to 80/20 lengthens the slot slightly and
  changes the answer from 13.3 to 12.8 per week.
- Pipeline dwell time. Plus or minus 25% moves the answer from 10.7 to 17.8 per
  week. Wide, but the inputs are primary and well measured, so the uncertainty
  band is much narrower than that in practice.
- The winter penalty proxy itself. Removing it entirely gives 9.0 per week, which
  is inside the stated 8 to 20 range either way.

## GCI sanity check at the Epique split

**What Epique publishes**, on [epiquerealty.com/join](https://epiquerealty.com/join):
an **85/15 split**, a monthly fee "as low as $99/month", a **0.1% transaction
fee**, and "NO Franchise Fees, NO Annual Fees, NO Desk Fees". Team caps are
published at $15K for family teams, $10K traditional, $5K power teams. The
**individual agent cap is not published** anywhere on the site, and neither
HousingWire nor RealTrends publishes it. A cap of $15,000 in year one declining
to $10,000 by year six, with a $500 per-transaction fee cap, circulates in
affiliate recruiting content ([theagentcollab.com](https://theagentcollab.com/epique-realty-understanding-caps-splits-and-fees-in-2024/))
and is an **UNVERIFIED INDUSTRY CLAIM**. Both outcomes are shown below.

Commission percentage: Redfin's transaction data puts the average buyer's agent
commission at **2.42%** in Q3 2025, and **2.32%** in the $500K to $999K band
([Redfin, Dec 8 2025](https://www.redfin.com/news/commissions-q3-2025/)). Redfin
does not publish a listing-side series. Wisconsin's mandatory
[WB-1](https://dsps.wi.gov/Documents/BoardCouncils/REB/Forms/WB-1.pdf) states on
lines 61-62 that "there is no standard market commission rate" and that
commissions are fully negotiable. 2.42% is used below.

    closings per year = 2.07 signed/mo x 85% survival x 12 = 21.1 sides
    average side      = $493,725 x 2.42%                    = $11,948
    gross commission  = 21.1 x $11,948                      = $252,100
    sales volume      = 21.1 x $493,725                     = $10,417,000

| Line | No cap (published 85/15 only) | With unverified $15K cap |
|---|---|---|
| Gross commission | $252,100 | $252,100 |
| Brokerage share | -$37,815 (15%) | -$15,000 (cap) |
| 0.1% transaction fee on volume | -$10,417 | -$10,417 |
| Tech fee, $99 x 12 | -$1,188 | -$1,188 |
| **Pre-expense income** | **$202,680** | **$225,495** |
| NAR median business expenses | -$9,530 | -$9,530 |
| **Net before tax** | **$193,150** | **$215,965** |

Business expense figure: [NAR 2026 Member Profile](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/even-in-a-tougher-market-realtors-are-holding-their-ground),
median $9,530.

**The sanity check fails in an instructive direction.** The same source puts
median REALTOR gross income at **$59,200** on **9 sides** and median individual
sales volume at **$2.7M**. This model produces 21 sides, $10.4M in volume, and
roughly $200K net. That is not a normal year for a solo agent. It is a strong
year for an established one.

If the actual income target is nearer the NAR median, the pipeline target is
nearer **3**, not 6, and the conversation requirement falls to roughly **7 per
week** in winter. Six in the pipe is a legitimate target, but it should be chosen
deliberately as a market-share ambition, not adopted because it sounds like a
round number.

## What it costs to produce a conversation

Largely unsourced, and stated plainly as such.

- **Direct mail.** The strongest evidence is the USPS 3% stated-intent ceiling for
  mail from a business with no prior relationship, and USPS notes intent
  overstates behavior. A 1,000-piece drop should be expected to produce well
  under 30 conversations, likely single digits. The frequently quoted "house file
  5-9%, prospect 2-4.4%, per ANA" pairing appears in **no ANA edition that could
  be verified** and is contradicted by the actual [ANA 2023 Response Rate
  Report](https://theworldsgreatestmarketing.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/rr-2024-02-ana-response-rate-report-2023.pdf),
  which reports 15.6% house and 10.8% prospect on sample sizes of 26 and 25 with
  no real estate segment at all. Treat both as unusable.
- **Open house visitors per open house.** No source at any tier.
- **Lofty pond reactivation.** No source. The 5,408-lead pond's conversation yield
  is unknown and is the single cheapest thing to measure directly rather than
  research.
- **Speed to response.** The only rigorous data is the MIT / InsideSales study
  (Prof. James Oldroyd, 6 companies, 15,000+ leads, 100,000+ call attempts):
  odds of qualifying a lead drop **21x** between a 5-minute and a 30-minute
  response, and contact odds fall roughly **10x** after the first hour.
  [Study PDF](https://25649.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na2.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf),
  [Lead Response Management](https://www.leadresponsemanagement.org/lrm_study/).
  The data was captured in 2007 and is **not real estate**, so treat the direction
  as solid and the magnitude as stale.

Given how little of this is measurable from published sources, the pond and the
open house system should be instrumented to produce these rates locally. Two
winters of the agent's own numbers would beat every source cited in this section.
