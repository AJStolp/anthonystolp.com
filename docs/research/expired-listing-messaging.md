# Messaging for expired-listing outreach: the realist position, and why it is the only one available

Date: 2026-08-12. Researcher: agent.
Companion to `direct-mail-costs-and-compliance.md`, which owns postage, list sourcing, vendor
pricing, cadence economics and the full Wisconsin compliance floor. **Nothing in those areas is
re-derived here.** This document covers only what to say and why.

Prompted by AJ: *"we need to come across a realist not a realtor as my boss likes to say. we
wanna be there to provide that data and inform them so they can make best informed decision for
themselves. we dont really wanna come from a angle of selling. that seems tacky."*

---

## The answer, up front

**The realist position is not a style preference. It is the only differentiated position left,
because every other agent has already taken the other one.**

An expired seller is the most solicited person in residential real estate. The evidence below
says they may hear from five agents the same morning, that they have heard the standard opener
"fifty times", and that they are actively hostile to it. A postcard that says some version of
*I would like to list your house* is not merely tacky. It is indistinguishable from the pile it
lands in.

Two corrections to earlier working assumptions, both of which change the plan:

1. **Speed-to-lead is NOT irrelevant for expireds.** I previously told AJ it was. That was
   wrong, and it matters. The first 24 to 48 hours is the highest-intent window.
2. **But AJ cannot win that window, and should not try.** His play is the second window, on the
   roughly half of sellers who do not relist quickly, where the noise has stopped.

---

## 1. What the seller is actually experiencing

Consistent across every vendor source reviewed:

- **Volume of contact.** Sellers "may receive calls from five other agents that morning,
  contributing to their defensiveness."
  ([Landvoice](https://www.landvoice.com/blog/what-to-say-when-calling-expired-listings-high-conversion-scripts-for-2026))
- **Specific scripts are burned out.** Sellers "have heard the 'I have a buyer' line fifty times
  already." Any opener that pattern-matches to it is discarded with it.
- **The emotional state is not neutral.** Reported as: feeling abandoned or misled by the
  previous agent, embarrassed the home did not sell when neighbours' did, and exhausted by the
  process. One source frames it precisely: sellers "aren't necessarily angry at individual
  agents but are angry at an industry that promised a result and delivered a 'For Sale' sign
  that collected dust."
  ([Luxury Presence](https://www.luxurypresence.com/blogs/playbook-for-expired-listings/))

**The implication is direct.** Embarrassment and a sense of having been oversold are the live
emotions. A card that arrives selling harder is not a neutral act. It re-triggers the exact
experience that just failed them. AJ's instinct that it "seems tacky" is understating it.

---

## 2. Why the realist position is evidenced, not just tasteful

The strongest support is not from the expired-listing vendors, who are selling dialers. It is
from NAR's own survey of what sellers say they choose an agent on:

| What sellers choose on | Share |
|---|---|
| Agent's reputation | 35% |
| **Honesty and trustworthiness** | **21%** |
| Referral from friend, neighbour or relative | more than one third found their agent this way |
| Found agent via internet search | 5% |

([NAR, *What Buyers, Sellers Want Most From Real Estate Agents*](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/sales-marketing/what-buyers-sellers-want-most-from-real-estate-agents))

Honesty and trustworthiness is the **second** most-cited selection criterion, and it rises to
33% among sellers aged 24 to 32. Dan's "realist not a realtor" framing is not a personality
quirk. It is selling directly into the attribute sellers report deciding on, to an audience
whose defining recent experience is feeling that someone was not straight with them.

The 5% internet-search figure is also worth holding onto: this audience is not going to find AJ
by searching. Being in their mailbox is how they encounter him at all.

## 3. The timing correction, and where the real opening is

This is the finding that reorders the plan.

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| First 24 to 48 hours is the highest-intent window | REDX, Landvoice |
| First agent to make contact has roughly **40% higher** chance of securing the listing than the fifth | Landvoice |
| **44.6%** of expired sellers relist within 30 to 35 days | REDX national MLS tracking |
| ~70% do **not** return to their original agent | REDX |
| 90 to 95% are eventually relisted and sold within six months | Mojo, Landvoice |

Read the second and third rows together. Roughly **45% of the opportunity is decided in the
first month, in a race AJ cannot enter.** He has a day job, the digest is daily rather than
instant, and the feed does not even carry an expiry date, only the date +Plus delivered the
record.

**But that leaves ~55% who have not relisted at 30 days, and 90 to 95% who eventually do.**
Those sellers have one useful property: by then, the phone has stopped ringing. Every agent
racing the 48-hour window has moved on to the next morning's expireds. REDX publishes an entire
["old expired listing" strategy](https://www.redx.com/blog/old-expired-listing-strategy/) on
exactly this, which is a fair signal that the quiet window is real, if also a signal that it is
becoming less quiet.

**So AJ's constraint and his positioning point at the same play.** He cannot be first. He can be
the one who shows up after the noise, saying something no one else said. Those are the same
strategy, which is a rare piece of luck.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** the data does not tell us how old these expirations are. `createTime`
is when +Plus delivered the record, not when the listing died. Whether the current feed is
arriving inside the 48-hour window or well outside it is unknown and worth checking against a
few known MLS numbers before leaning hard on the late-window theory.

## 4. Source quality, honestly

Most of section 1 and 3 comes from **companies that sell expired-listing data and dialers**:
REDX, Vulcan7, Landvoice, Mojo. They have a direct commercial interest in expireds looking like
a goldmine and in urgency being paramount. Treat their conversion and response figures as
directional at best.

The exception is **NAR**, which surveys sellers rather than selling to agents, and is the basis
for section 2. Where the two disagree, prefer NAR.

`direct-mail-costs-and-compliance.md` §6.3 already established the related point that the
ubiquitous "7 to 12 touches" figure has **no traceable source** and is industry convention. The
same skepticism applies to the response-rate benchmarks quoted around expired mail. Do not build
a forecast on them.

## 5. What this means for the piece

### The strategic idea

**Give them the honest read on why it did not sell, with no ask attached.**

This is available to AJ and to almost nobody else, because the triage already generates it per
lead: list price against estimated value, who marketed it and how, tenure and equity, and what
the previous agent's own remarks reveal. On a live sample it produced things like a listing
priced 39% above estimated value, and another whose entire marketing copy was "Coming soon!".

That is a genuinely useful artefact for someone who has just been through a failure and has not
been told why. Offering it, and meaning it even if they never list, is the realist position made
concrete rather than merely claimed.

### What the card must not do

- **Not ask for the listing.** The ask is the thing every other card makes.
- **Not claim a buyer.** Burned out, and usually untrue.
- **Not diagnose on the card itself.** Telling someone in writing that their home was overpriced,
  unsolicited, is a poor first contact and risks reading as an insult rather than help. Offer the
  read; deliver it in conversation.
- **Not describe the property as being for sale.** 452.136(3), and there is no listing firm to
  consent since the listing expired. See the compliance companion, §4.3.

### What the card must carry

Taken from `direct-mail-costs-and-compliance.md` §4.2 and §4.7, not re-derived:

- The firm's name **exactly as printed on the Wisconsin license**, or a DSPS-filed trade name.
  **Unresolved: issue #40.**
- That name **clear and conspicuous** — 452.136(2)(b). This rules out burying it as one line of
  a small handwritten signature block, which is what the first draft of the mailer template did.
- Clear indication the firm is a **business enterprise, not a private party** — 452.136(2)(a).
- "ExSell Experts" never without "Epique Realty", and not out-shouting it.
- AJ's status as a real estate professional readily apparent — NAR Article 12. Wisconsin itself
  does not require the salesperson's name; NAR does.
- Advertising happens **in the name of and under the supervision of the firm** — 452.136(2)(b).
  **Correction:** an earlier draft of this document called per-piece broker pre-approval a
  compliance gate. It is not. The statute requires the firm's name and firm supervision; it
  does not mandate that a broker approve each piece. Whether Epique requires that is
  **brokerage policy**, and `direct-mail-costs-and-compliance.md` §4.7 already records that no
  published Epique marketing policy could be found and that this is a question for the broker
  rather than an assumption. Ask it in the same message as the firm-name question.
  The reason brokers usually do care: exposure for a non-compliant ad runs to the firm and the
  supervising broker, not only to the salesperson.
- Image rights cleared. Not MLS photos by default.

### Tracking

`direct-mail-costs-and-compliance.md` §6.5 already recommends every piece drive to a tracked
destination, and the site's `/n/[token]` offline attribution exists for exactly this. thanks.io's
send-postcard API accepts a **`qrcode_url`** field, so this is a config value, not a build.
Without it the response rate is guessed rather than measured.

## 6. Recommended copy

One recommendation rather than a menu. 439 characters, which fits a 4x6 handwritten card.

Edited once for dead prose: every hedge that carried no information was cut, taking it from 519
to 439 characters without losing a single fact. "If that would be useful, I will send it over"
became "Say the word and I will send it"; "and no obligation" went because the this-year-next-
year-or-never clause already says it better.

> Hi {first},
>
> Your {city} home came off the market without selling. I am not writing to ask for the listing.
>
> I looked at what it was priced against and what actually sold near it. Say the word and I will
> send it. No charge, whether you list again this year, next year or never.
>
> Or tell me what you were trying to do. I would rather hear that.
>
> Anthony Stolp, {firm}

Why each part is there:

| Line | Job |
|---|---|
| "came off the market without selling" | Neutral and factual. Not "expired", which sounds like a verdict. States the fact rather than hedging with "recently". |
| **"I am not writing to ask for the listing"** | The whole differentiator, in the second sentence, before they stop reading. Nobody else says it. |
| "what it was priced against and what actually sold near it" | Data, specific, and plainly deliverable. Not a vague offer of "a free market analysis". |
| "whether you list again this year, next year or never" | Removes the implied obligation. This is the line that makes the no-ask claim credible instead of a technique. |
| **"I would rather hear that"** | AJ's actual objective: learn their journey. Says he would prefer their story to his own analysis, which is the relationship-first position stated rather than claimed. |

Notes: no em dashes, per house style. No advice, only information, per the educate-not-advise
rule. Firm name goes in the card **design** as well as the signature to satisfy "clear and
conspicuous", which is a layout requirement the handwritten message alone cannot meet.

## 6a. What the card actually looks like, from a live render

Rendered through the real thanks.io API with `preview: true`, which returns finished images
without mailing or charging. Three findings that only showed up on the render:

1. **Paragraphs need blank lines.** Joining the message with single newlines produced one dense
   unreadable block. The handwriting engine honours `\n\n` as a paragraph break, and the
   legibility difference is large. Fixed in `nodes/mailer-build-postcard.js`; the signature
   block still uses single newlines so it stays tight.
2. **Handwriting style matters more than expected, and the descriptions oversell.** Style 12
   ("Wow! Signal", described as a "neat casual script-print mix with clear forms") renders as a
   thin loopy script that is genuinely hard to read at 439 characters. **Style 104 ("Analytic
   Atom") is the pick**: clean handwritten print, highly legible, reads as a person rather than
   a font.
3. **`handwriting_realism` only applies to the AI-type styles** (ids 101 and up). On the
   "Realistic" styles (1 to 20) the flag is a no-op. Style 104 is an AI type, so realism is
   live there. There are 36 styles total, retrievable from `GET /handwriting-styles`, which
   thanks.io does not document anywhere.

The top half of the rendered interior is blank. That is the fold: 1650x2475 at 300dpi is
5.5 x 8.25 inches, so the upper panel is the inside front of a top-fold card and the message
sits on the lower writing surface. Worth confirming on the first physical piece.
